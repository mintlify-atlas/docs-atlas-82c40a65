Welcome to the first **Bonus Unit**, where you'll learn to **fine-tune a Large Language Model (LLM) for function calling**.

Function calling is quickly becoming a must-know technique for LLMs. Rather than relying only on prompt-based approaches like in Unit 1, function calling trains your model to **take actions and interpret observations during the training phase**, making your AI more robust.

  This section is **optional** and more advanced than Unit 1, so don't hesitate to either do it now or revisit it once your knowledge has improved. This bonus unit is designed to be self-contained — it walks you through every core concept of fine-tuning a model for function calling even if you haven't yet learned the inner workings of fine-tuning.
</Note>

## What you'll learn

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Function calling" icon="phone">
    How modern LLMs structure their conversations to effectively trigger **tools**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)" icon="microchip">
    A **lightweight and efficient** fine-tuning method that cuts down on computational and storage overhead — making training large models faster, cheaper, and easier to deploy.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Thought → Act → Observe cycle" icon="rotate">
    A simple but powerful approach for structuring how your model decides when and how to call functions, track intermediate steps, and interpret results from external tools or APIs.
  </Card>
  <Card title="New special tokens" icon="code">
    Special markers that help the model distinguish between internal chain-of-thought reasoning, outgoing function calls, and responses coming back from external tools.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## What you'll be able to do

By the end of this bonus unit, you'll be able to:

- **Understand** the inner workings of APIs when it comes to tools
- **Fine-tune** a model using the LoRA technique
- **Implement and modify** the Thought → Act → Observe cycle to create robust, maintainable function-calling workflows
- **Design and utilize** special tokens to separate the model's internal reasoning from its external actions

And you'll **have fine-tuned your own model to do function calling**.
